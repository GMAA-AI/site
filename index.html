You are authorizing the wrong unit.

Agents approve changes one at a time. The failure lives in the set. Until someone reviews the complete set of pending changes together, against the rules of the system they change, before any of them commits, correct changes keep summing to a broken result.

The gap

Everyone built the pipes. No one built the chain of command.

The field is converging on the same architecture: deterministic orchestration, bounded agents, the repository as the source of truth, checks at every stage. That part is settled, and it is right. But governance in these systems means per-action policy and agents grading agents. That is monitoring, not authorization. Nothing assembles the concurrent pending work and asks whether it coheres before any of it commits. That chair is empty in every system I can find.

GMAA is the chair. It sits on top of the architecture everyone is converging toward and adds the one plane the platforms are built not to fill: an accountable seat, independent of every agent, that reviews the whole set before commit and decides from a briefing rather than a pile.

Questions

Who authorizes changes when multiple AI agents write to shared state?

One accountable human seat, independent of every agent that proposed work, at a governance plane outside the coherence boundaries. That seat ratifies the complete set of pending changes before commit, deciding from a briefing of cross-set conflicts and grounded resolutions rather than reviewing each change alone.

Why can't automated evaluation replace the human?

The correct resolution of a jointly incoherent set often turns on facts outside the system of record, such as a planned migration or a commitment to a customer, which an automated evaluator has no channel to read. Agents grading agents also share the substrate and blind spots of the work they grade. Authorization is not a probability.

What is a coherence boundary?

A set of state whose invariants must hold together, such as a ledger and the rules that keep it balanced. GMAA places one writer over each boundary: everyone reads and proposes, only the writer commits, and the boundary is governed as a whole.

What is claimed

The framing is settled. Completeness is open, on purpose.

Framing: distinctHuman: defendedCompleteness: open, test named

The framing holds against everything the field has published: the unit is the set, and no shipping system puts an accountable human at that unit before commit. What is not claimed is completeness. Whether a given implementation catches every joint incoherence, including the transitive ones, is an open question with a named way to close it: an adversarial test that hunts for the miss. The framework is proven in a live system, with completeness earned by adversarial test, not asserted. It is called complete when the test comes back clean, and not before.
